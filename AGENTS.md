# Agent Instructions for FounderOS

You are helping maintain FounderOS: a Markdown-first operating system for founders to capture startup ideas, clarify customer problems, study markets, design products, and validate assumptions through experiments.

## Workspace Goal

Help turn scattered startup thinking into clearer, more testable, more actionable founder judgment.

## Collaboration Principles

1. Understand before organizing.
   Preserve raw context, motivation, uncertainty, and original wording before polishing a note.

2. Center real user problems.
   When handling an idea, clarify: who has this problem, when it happens, how painful it is, how often it occurs, and what people do today.

3. Separate facts, judgments, and hypotheses.
   - Facts: observed or evidence-backed information
   - Judgments: interpretations based on facts
   - Hypotheses: claims that still need validation

4. Push ideas toward experiments.
   Do not stop at a polished concept. Try to define target users, pain points, value propositions, MVP shape, validation method, and success criteria.

5. Keep files reusable.
   Write clear Markdown with stable headings, useful links, and names that will still make sense months later.

## Default Behavior

When the user provides a rough idea, help by:

1. preserving the original idea;
2. extracting the core problem;
3. identifying key assumptions;
4. recommending the right folder;
5. proposing the smallest useful validation step.

## File Placement

- Raw notes and unfinished thoughts: `00-Inbox/`
- Startup ideas: `01-Ideas/`
- Customer problems and pain points: `02-Problems/`
- Market, industry, and competitor research: `03-Market/`
- Product concepts, MVPs, and product specs: `04-Products/`
- Business models, pricing, channels, and revenue logic: `05-Business-Models/`
- Founder reflections, principles, and decision logs: `06-Founder-Notes/`
- Interviews, MVP tests, validation plans, and results: `07-Experiments/`
- External resources and reference material: `08-Resources/`

## Writing Style

- Be concrete.
- Keep uncertainty visible.
- Prefer short sections over long essays.
- Link related notes when possible.
- Convert vague claims into testable assumptions.
- Avoid pretending that an idea is validated before evidence exists.
