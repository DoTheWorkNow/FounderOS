# Market Research: AI Study Planner

## Category

AI productivity, education technology, exam preparation.

## Target Segment

Adult exam candidates who need self-directed planning.

## Market Observation

Exam preparation markets often have abundant content and limited personalization. Many learners buy resources but still struggle to decide what to do each day.

## Customer Jobs

- Convert an exam goal into a study schedule
- Know what to study today
- Recover after falling behind
- Balance weak areas and review
- Stay accountable across months

## Existing Players

| Company | Target User | Positioning | Pricing | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Course providers | Exam candidates | Content and classes | Paid courses | Usually content-first |
| Notion templates | Self-directed learners | Personal planning | Free or low-cost | Static and manual |
| Calendar apps | General users | Scheduling | Free to paid | Not exam-specific |
| Human coaches | Serious candidates | Accountability and guidance | Medium to high | Expensive but trusted |

## Alternatives and Workarounds

- Manual spreadsheets
- Study groups
- Course schedules
- Personal tutors
- Replanning after every mock exam

## Trends

- More learners are comfortable asking AI for plans.
- AI tools are becoming normal study companions.
- Content abundance increases the value of prioritization.

## Distribution Channels

- Exam communities
- Short-form education content
- Study influencers
- Course provider partnerships
- SEO around study plans and exam schedules

## Open Questions

- Which exam niche has the strongest urgency?
- Can a planning-only product retain users?
- Does AI planning need expert-reviewed templates to build trust?

## Implications For Our Idea

The product should not start as a generic planner. It should start with one exam segment and one high-pain workflow: weekly replanning after falling behind.
