# Idea: AI Study Planner

## One-Sentence Description

An AI study planner that helps exam candidates turn vague goals into weekly study plans, daily tasks, and review loops.

## Original Context

Many students do not fail because they lack study material. They fail because they cannot translate a large exam goal into a realistic plan and keep adjusting it after falling behind.

## Target User

Adult learners preparing for competitive exams while working or attending school.

## User Problem

They have limited time, inconsistent energy, and too many resources. They do not know what to study today, what to skip, and how to recover after missing several days.

## Existing Alternatives

- Generic calendar apps
- Static study plans from courses
- Excel trackers
- Coaching communities
- Manual notes in Notion or Obsidian

## Value Proposition

Create a realistic plan, adapt it weekly, and keep the learner focused on the next useful action.

## Core Assumptions

- Users feel planning pain strongly enough to try a new tool.
- Adaptive planning is more valuable than another content library.
- Users will share exam date, available hours, and current progress.
- Weekly adjustment creates more retention than one-time plan generation.

## Evidence So Far

No direct evidence yet. This needs interviews with exam candidates.

## Possible MVP

A simple web form where users enter exam date, subjects, available weekly hours, and current level. The MVP returns a 7-day plan and a weekly review checklist.

## Validation Experiment

Interview 10 target users and manually create 3 study plans to see whether users find the output valuable enough to use for one week.

## Risks and Uncertainties

- Users may prefer human coaches.
- Users may not trust generated plans.
- Planning may be less painful than motivation or content quality.
- Different exams may require very different planning logic.

## Next Action

Run 10 user interviews before building the product.
